Why I Built a Breathing Space With No Instructions

A story about silence, James Turrell, and the courage to not guide.

Every breathing app I found wanted to tell me what to do.

Breathe in for 4 seconds. Hold for 7. Breathe out for 8. Follow the animation. Listen to the voice. Count your breaths. Tap when you're done. Rate your session. Sign up to save your progress.

I didn't want any of that. I wanted a space where I could just breathe — not because something told me to, but because I walked in and my body remembered how.

So I built one. It's at breath.

There's a circle. It breathes — slowly, steadily, on its own. You can watch it. You can match it. You can ignore it and breathe your own rhythm. You can press and hold to pause. You can long-press to slow it down. Nobody tells you what to do.

The circle doesn't guide you. It keeps you company.

I was thinking about James Turrell — the artist who makes rooms where you can't tell if the wall is solid or if it's an opening to the sky. His Perceptual Cells don't tell you what to see. They create a condition where seeing becomes the experience.

I wanted that. Not an app that instructs, but a space that holds.

So here's what happens:

You open the page. For the first 15 seconds, a faint text appears at the bottom — just enough to tell you this exists and what it is. Then it fades. 25 seconds later, it's gone. After that, there's nothing but you and the circle.

If you stay long enough, a small heart icon appears. It's optional. If you tap it, your webcam turns on — locally, in your browser, nothing uploaded anywhere — and it reads the subtle color changes in your face to detect your pulse. You see your heart rate alongside the breathing circle. Your body and the circle, side by side. Two rhythms. One conversation.

When you're ready to leave, a prompt asks: 帶走嗎？ — "Want to take it with you?"

You can download a small chart of your breathing session as an SVG file. A thin ring of light fades out around the circle — a quiet goodbye. Then it's over. No "Great job!" No streak counter. No "Come back tomorrow!"

You were here. You breathed. That's the whole thing.

I later found out that someone else — Daniela Baron — independently built something similar for the same reasons. She was tired of subscriptions, ads, and "vague spiritual platitudes and ambient whale sounds." She built a clean, simple breathing timer with voice prompts and deployed it to GitHub Pages.

Her app tells you when to breathe. Mine doesn't. I think both are right. Some people want guidance. Some people want space. There should be tools for both.
